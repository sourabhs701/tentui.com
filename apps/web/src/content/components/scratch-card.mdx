## Features

- A brushed metal foil in `silver`, `gold`, or `graphite`, with fine grain, a
  diagonal sheen, and an engraved hint that scratches away with the rest.
- A soft round brush erases the foil the moment the pointer moves. Strokes fill
  in between pointer events, so fast flicks leave no gaps.
- Coverage is counted on a small grid as you scratch, without reading pixels
  back from the canvas. Once `threshold` of the foil is gone, the rest fades
  and lifts away and the content settles into place.
- Tiny foil specks pop off the brush, and the sheen drifts a little with the
  pointer.
- The scratch is filtered noise that gets louder and brighter the faster you
  move, and goes silent when you stop. A light haptic ticks as you scratch,
  and the reveal plays a two-note chime with a firmer pulse.
- At rest nothing animates or draws, so the card is perfectly still.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ScratchCard } from "@/components/scratch-card";
```

```tsx
<ScratchCard aria-label="Scratch to reveal">
  <p>You found it</p>
</ScratchCard>
```

The card fills the width of its container up to 320 px and keeps a 28:17
aspect ratio. Set a different width with `className`, for example
`max-w-[280px]`. Content taller than the card is clipped.

### Gold foil with a prize

```tsx
<ScratchCard
  foil="gold"
  hint="Scratch to win"
  aria-label="Gold scratch card"
  onReveal={() => claimPrize()}
>
  <div className="text-center">
    <p className="text-xs uppercase">You won</p>
    <p className="font-semibold text-3xl">20% off</p>
  </div>
</ScratchCard>
```

### Sound off

Turn the sound off and keep the haptics, or the other way round:

```tsx
<ScratchCard foil="graphite" sound={false}>
  <p>Free shipping</p>
</ScratchCard>
```

### Reset and progress

`onProgress` reports the cleared fraction from 0 to 1, and `onReveal` fires
once per reveal. Put a fresh foil back with the `reset()` method on a ref, or
remount the card by changing its `key`. `reveal()` uncovers it at once.

```tsx
const card = useRef<ScratchCardHandle>(null);
const [progress, setProgress] = useState(0);

<ScratchCard ref={card} onProgress={setProgress} onReveal={() => track()}>
  <p>{Math.round(progress * 100)}% cleared</p>
</ScratchCard>
<button onClick={() => card.current?.reset()}>Reset</button>
```

When the card is resized mid-scratch, the foil is repainted at the new size
and what was scratched off is stretched to match, so the cleared fraction is
kept.

## Interaction

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Drag on the foil | Scratches it away; past `threshold`, the rest lifts off |
| **Reveal** button | Reveals the content at once |
| `Enter` / `Space` on the foil | Reveals the content at once |

The **Reveal** button sits in the bottom corner. It shows when it has keyboard
focus, while a mouse hovers over the card, and always on touch screens. It
hides while you scratch. Once the card is revealed it stays revealed until
it's reset.

Only one pointer scratches at a time: a second finger and a right click are
ignored. Losing the pointer ends the stroke, and a reveal in the middle of a
stroke ends it cleanly.

## Sound and haptics

While you scratch, short bursts of filtered noise play at most every 28 ms.
Each one fades in and out, so strokes never click at the start or end. Faster
strokes are louder and brighter, and rubbing over card that has already been
cleared is quieter. Every 44 px scratched over foil triggers a `selection`
haptic. The reveal plays a soft two-note chime with a `medium` haptic.

Sound and haptics only follow user input. Mounting the card, `reset()`, and
`reveal()` are silent, and nothing plays while the tab is hidden. Turn each
off with `sound={false}` and `haptics={false}`.

## Accessibility

The card is a `group` named by `aria-label` (`"Scratch card"` by default) or
`aria-labelledby`. Scratching is a path-based gesture, so the **Reveal**
button is the single-pointer and keyboard equivalent
([WCAG 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)).
It is always in the tab order and the accessibility tree.

Until the card is revealed, the content is `aria-hidden` and `inert`, so
screen readers can't read it early and its links can't take focus. On reveal
a polite live region announces "Revealed", the content becomes available, and
focus moves to it if it was on the card. The focus ring shows for keyboard
focus only. Dragging on the foil never scrolls the page on touch screens. A
disabled card is dimmed and can't be scratched or revealed.

With reduced motion enabled no specks fly off, the sheen stays put, and the
reveal swaps instantly. Sound and haptics still follow their props, because
they confirm the scratch rather than decorate movement.
