## Features

- A recessed tray and a squircle thumb built from stacked CSS and SVG
  layers: a milky or smoked glass body, a bevel band lit from the top-left
  with a glint where the light leaves, a crisp top rim, a soft caustic on the
  tray, and a translucent shadow. No WebGL and no canvas.
- A neon rim light floats a hairline outside the glass, with a white-hot core
  and a soft bloom that spills onto the page like a small lamp. Off it hugs the
  bottom-left in cool lilac; on it hugs the top-right in warm amber, and it
  tints the inside of the glass on the side it's on.
- One progress value drives everything. The thumb springs across with a little
  overshoot and squashes with speed, the moon's pixels fly apart and land as a
  sun, and the light runs half a lap round the thumb, always the same way, a
  beat behind it. The bloom warms up as the thumb lands.
- Drag the glass to scrub the whole flip, both ways. Let go past the middle,
  or flick it, to flip; let go on the starting side and nothing happens.
- Hovering with a mouse turns the light up, and the top sheen leans toward the
  pointer as if the pointer were the light.
- Plays a glass tap with a faint ring and a light haptic on every flip.
- At rest nothing moves and nothing renders per frame. While it moves, React
  doesn't re-render: the thumb, light, and pixels are written directly.

## Usage

```tsx
import { GlassToggle } from "@/components/glass-toggle";
```

```tsx
<GlassToggle label="Lamp" />
```

Use `checked` and `onCheckedChange` to control it. `onCheckedChange` fires
once per flip, the moment the switch commits: on a click or key press, or when
a drag is let go.

```tsx
const [enabled, setEnabled] = useState(false);

<GlassToggle label="Night light" checked={enabled} onCheckedChange={setEnabled} />
```

The `sm` size is the same switch drawn smaller, for dense lists. Turn the
sound off and keep the haptics, or the other way round:

```tsx
<GlassToggle label="Auto-dim" size="sm" sound={false} />
```

Give it a `name` to submit `"on"` or `"off"` with a native form. The two light
colors are CSS variables, so you can retheme them without a prop:

```tsx
<GlassToggle
  label="Lamp"
  className="[--glass-toggle-off:#7dd3fc] [--glass-toggle-on:#fb7185]"
/>
```

### Driving a theme (optional)

The switch only reports `checked`; it knows nothing about themes. To use it as
a light/dark switch, pass your theme setter to `onCheckedChange`. TentUI's own
header control is [`theme-toggle`](/components/theme-toggle), a different
component.

```tsx
const { resolvedTheme, setTheme } = useTheme();

<GlassToggle
  label="Light mode"
  checked={resolvedTheme === "light"}
  onCheckedChange={(light) => setTheme(light ? "light" : "dark")}
/>
```

## Interaction

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Click or tap | Flips the switch |
| `Space` / `Enter` | Flips the switch |
| Drag the glass | Scrubs the thumb, pixels, and light; letting go past the middle flips it |
| Flick the glass | Flips it in the flick's direction, wherever it's let go |

Past either end the thumb rubber-bands. A vertical swipe still scrolls the
page. Losing the pointer, the window, or the tab mid-drag lets go without
flipping, and a second finger is ignored while the first one drags.

## Sound and haptics

Each flip plays a short, bright glass tap with a faint pitched ring, a little
higher and brighter for on than off, and a `light` haptic. They fire the
moment the switch commits, not when the thumb lands. Dragging is silent, and a
drag let go on its starting side plays nothing.

Sound and haptics only follow user input. The first render and changes to the
`checked` prop are silent, and nothing plays while the tab is hidden. Turn
each off with `sound={false}` and `haptics={false}`.

## Accessibility

The switch is a `button` with `role="switch"` and `aria-checked`, named by
`label`, `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby`, and described by
`aria-describedby`. The tray, glass, glyphs, and light are hidden from
assistive technology. The focus ring shows for keyboard focus only. The hit
area is at least 44 px tall at both sizes. The state never relies on color
alone: the thumb's side and the glyph's shape change too. A disabled switch is
dimmed, loses its bloom, and can't be focused or flipped.

With reduced motion enabled the thumb moves at once with no overshoot or
squash, the glyph swaps without scattering, and the light jumps to its resting
place and color. Hover and press still brighten the light. Sound and haptics
stay on because they confirm the switch rather than decorate movement.
