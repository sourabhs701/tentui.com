## Features

- Brush across the row with a mouse, finger, or pen. A tube rings each time
  the pointer crosses its center, and the pointer speed at the crossing sets
  how loud, how bright, and how wide the swing is.
- Each tube is a damped pendulum. It swings up to about 35 degrees, rings, and
  settles back to vertical. One `requestAnimationFrame` loop runs the whole row
  in fixed 1/120 s steps and goes to sleep once every tube is at rest.
- A swinging tube pushes its neighbour with a quarter of its own impulse, so a
  brush rolls down the row as a ripple. A tube can't ring again within 130 ms.
- Notes come from a scale, with the longest tube on the left playing the
  lowest note. The default is C major pentatonic across about 1.5 octaves.
- `material` changes both the ring and the look: metal rings for about
  2.5 s, bamboo knocks for half a second, and glass rings high and pure for
  about 3 s.
- `wind` lets slow gusts sway and ring the tubes on their own, with a button
  to pause them.
- Plays a soft, selection, or medium haptic by strike strength on supported
  phones.

## Usage

```tsx
import { WindChimes } from "@/components/wind-chimes";
```

```tsx
<WindChimes />
```

Pick the tuning with `scale` and `root`. Named scales (`pentatonic`,
`hirajoshi`, `whole-tone`, and `major`) are spread across about 1.5 octaves
above `root`. An array of semitone offsets is used as written, one note per
tube, and repeats up the octaves when there are more tubes than offsets. An
invalid `root` falls back to C4.

```tsx
<WindChimes count={7} scale="hirajoshi" root="D4" material="glass" />
<WindChimes scale={[0, 3, 5, 7, 10]} root="A3" showNotes />
```

`onStrike` fires for every note, with the tube index, the note name, the
strength from 0 to 1, and whether the strike came from the pointer, the
keyboard, or the wind.

```tsx
<WindChimes onStrike={({ note, velocity }) => console.log(note, velocity)} />
```

Set `wind` to let gusts move the row. `windStrength` sets how hard and how
often it blows. The component renders a "Pause wind" button under the row
while the wind is on.

```tsx
<WindChimes wind windStrength={0.5} />
```

Set `size` to `sm`, `md`, or `lg`. The row also shrinks to fit its container.
On phones, keep `count` at 7 or fewer: five tubes keep hit zones of at least
36 px at a 390 px wide viewport, while nine tubes get narrower ones.

`sway` adds a barely visible, silent idle sway, which the preview at the top
of this page uses.

## How the sound is built

There are no audio files. Each strike plays a short voice from a small Web
Audio synth inside the component:

- Five sine partials at the ratios 1 : 2.76 : 5.40 : 8.93 : 13.34 of a struck
  tube, each with a 2 ms attack and an exponential decay. Higher partials
  decay faster, and `material` sets the decay times and partial levels.
- A 7 ms triangle tick for the strike transient.
- Loudness follows the square of the strike strength, so soft brushes stay
  quiet, and each note is detuned by up to 0.6% so repeats differ slightly.
- A short low-passed echo, about 90 ms with a feedback of 0.25, mixed in at
  0.12. Turn it off with `reverb={false}`.

All instances share one `AudioContext`, a compressor, and a cap of 12 live
voices, so a quick flick across several rows doesn't clip. When the cap is
hit, the oldest voice fades out early. A tube struck again while it is still
ringing fades its previous note over 30 ms first. `volume` sets the level of
each row.

## iOS audio and haptics

Browsers keep audio locked until the page gets a click, tap, or key press.
The `AudioContext` is created and resumed inside the first such gesture, never
during render or on the server. Until then, the chimes still swing but stay
silent. Hovering alone doesn't unlock audio, so on desktop a brush plays
sound once the visitor has clicked somewhere on the page. On iOS Safari, the
first tap on the chimes unlocks audio.

Haptics use `web-haptics`, which works in Safari on iOS 18 and later and in
Chrome on Android. Elsewhere they do nothing. Haptics only follow pointer and
keyboard strikes, never the wind, and are limited to one every 45 ms across
the row.

No sound or haptics play on first render, in a hidden tab, or while the
chimes are scrolled off-screen. When a hidden tab comes back, the tubes are
reset to rest rather than catching up. If Web Audio is blocked or missing,
the chimes still animate without errors. Turn each output off on its own with
`sound={false}` and `haptics={false}`.

## Keyboard

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `Tab` | Moves focus into the row, onto the last focused tube |
| `←` / `→` | Moves focus to the previous or next tube |
| `Home` / `End` | Moves focus to the first or last tube |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Strikes the focused tube at medium strength |

Clicking or tapping a single tube also strikes it at medium strength.

## Accessibility

The row is a `group` named "Wind chimes" by default; pass `aria-label` to name
it. Each tube is a real button named "Chime 1 of 5", with the note added when
`showNotes` is set. The row is a single tab stop, so every note can be played
from the keyboard, and brushing is an extra on top.

Set `announce` to read the note of the last strike through a polite live
region. It waits for the row to go quiet, so a ripple is announced once, not
once per tube.

The row sets `touch-action: pan-y`, so a horizontal brush plays the chimes
and a vertical swipe still scrolls the page.

With reduced motion enabled, a strike is a small, quick twitch of about
6 degrees, and there is no idle sway or wind. Sound and haptics stay on
because they respond to input rather than decorate movement.
