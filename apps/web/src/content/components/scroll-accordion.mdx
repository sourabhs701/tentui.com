## Features

- An accordion you open by reading. Scroll the panel and each section's
  header slides up and stays: read sections collect at the top edge, the
  ones ahead wait at the bottom, and the section you're reading fills the
  space between them.
- No scroll-jacking and no height animation. It is native scrolling, so
  wheel, trackpad momentum, touch, scrollbar, and keyboard all behave as
  usual. The stacking comes from `position: sticky` with both a top and a
  bottom offset on every header, so content never reflows while you read.
- Two looks. `deck` overlaps the stacks like a pile of index cards, with a
  12 px sliver of each stacked card showing and deeper cards slightly
  smaller, so many sections still leave a roomy reading band. The next card
  slides up over the open one, like a card dealt onto a pile. `list` keeps
  every header as a full row, so the stacks double as a readable outline.
- A small ring on each header fills as you read that section: read sections
  show a full ring with a dot, the open one fills as you go, and the ones
  ahead are empty.
- Press any header, stacked or not, to glide to that section. Its header
  lands exactly in its slot with the section starting just below it.
- Gentle `snap`: a scroll that ends near the start of a section settles
  onto it, while long sections stay free to stop anywhere.
- The last section always has room to open, however short it is.
- Every visual is drawn from the scroll position once per frame and writes
  only `transform`, `opacity`, a ring's `stroke-dashoffset`, and a data
  attribute, so scrolling never triggers layout work.
- A header press glides on an ease-out curve, 300 to 620 ms depending on the
  distance. Wheel, touch, or a key press stops it at once and hands the
  scroll back to you.

## Usage

```tsx
import { ScrollAccordion } from "@/components/scroll-accordion";
```

Pass the sections as `items`, each with an `id`, a `title`, optional `meta`
text, and its `content`. Give the panel a `height`: the sections scroll
inside it.

```tsx
<ScrollAccordion
  aria-label="Pour-over guide"
  height={380}
  items={[
    { id: "grind", title: "Grind", meta: "0:00", content: <p>…</p> },
    { id: "bloom", title: "Bloom", meta: "0:30", content: <p>…</p> },
    { id: "pour", title: "Pour", meta: "1:15", content: <p>…</p> },
  ]}
/>
```

Use `variant="list"` for flush rows where every header stays readable, for
example policies, settings, or docs:

```tsx
<ScrollAccordion variant="list" items={sections} height={360} />
```

Control the open section with `value` and `onValueChange`. Setting `value`
from outside scrolls the panel there. `onValueChange` fires once per change,
including once for a header press rather than for every section passed on
the way:

```tsx
const [value, setValue] = useState("overview");

<ScrollAccordion items={sections} value={value} onValueChange={setValue} />;
```

To scroll from your own code without a controlled value, use the ref:

```tsx
const ref = useRef<ScrollAccordionHandle>(null);

ref.current?.scrollToItem("retention");
```

With a full-height `peek` (the list default) every stacked header takes a
full row, so keep `items.length × spineHeight` well under the `height`.
Style the parts with their data attributes: `data-slot="scroll-accordion"`
with `data-variant`, `scroll-accordion-header` and `scroll-accordion-content`
with `data-state` (`read`, `active`, or `upcoming`), `data-stack` (`top` or
`bottom`) on headers, and `data-complete` on headers whose section has been
read through.

## Interaction

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Scroll, swipe, or drag the scrollbar | Reads through the sections; headers stack as you pass them |
| Press a header | Glides to that section |
| `Tab` | Moves through the headers and any links or controls in the sections |
| `Enter` or `Space` on a header | Glides to that section |
| `↑` `↓` `Page Up` `Page Down` `Home` `End` | Scroll the panel, as in any scroll area |

## Sound and haptics

When the open section changes because you scrolled or pressed a header, the
panel plays a short, dry paper flip, at most once every 45 ms, so a long
jump riffles through the cards like a thumb along a deck. On touch screens
each flip also triggers a `selection` haptic. Nothing plays on mount, on
resize, or when `value` changes from your code. Turn them off with
`sound={false}` and `haptics={false}`.

## Accessibility

Each header is a real `button` inside a heading (`h3` by default, set with
`headingLevel`), and each section is a `region` labelled by its header. The
open section's header has `aria-current="true"`. The sections are never
hidden, so screen readers read the whole document in order and `aria-expanded`
isn't used. Pass `aria-label` to name the panel as a region.

Focus never hides under a stack: when `Tab` moves into a link inside a
section, the panel scrolls so the link sits in the reading band. The focus
ring is drawn on the header's card, and a header you `Tab` to lifts above its
stack so its whole card shows, not just its sliver. The progress rings are decorative and
hidden from assistive technology.

With reduced motion enabled, pressing a header jumps to the section instead
of gliding. The stacking and rings follow your own scrolling, so they stay.

## FAQ

### How do I install the Scroll Accordion with shadcn?

Run `npx shadcn@latest add @tentui/scroll-accordion` after adding the
`@tentui` registry to `components.json` ([installation guide](/docs/installation)).
The CLI copies `scroll-accordion.tsx` into your project and installs
`@rexa-developer/tiks` and `web-haptics`.

### How is a scroll accordion different from a regular accordion?

A regular accordion hides closed panels and animates their height when you
open one. The Scroll Accordion keeps every section in the page and opens them
as you scroll: headers stick and stack at the top and bottom edges, so you
always see where you are, and the content never jumps.

### Is the Scroll Accordion accessible to keyboard and screen reader users?

Yes. Headers are buttons inside headings that you reach with `Tab` and
activate with `Enter` or `Space`, sections are labelled regions, and the open
section's header has `aria-current`. Focused links are scrolled clear of the
stacked headers.

### Can I control which section is open?

Yes. Pass `value` and `onValueChange` to keep the open section in React
state; setting `value` scrolls the panel to that section. You can also call
`scrollToItem(id)` on the ref.

### Can I show every header title in the stacks?

Yes. Use `variant="list"`, or set `peek` to the `spineHeight`, and every
stacked header stays a full, readable row. The deck's default 12 px `peek`
overlaps them like index cards instead, which leaves more room for reading.

### Is the Scroll Accordion free to use?

Yes. The Scroll Accordion is free and source-available: the shadcn CLI copies
its source into your project, and you can use it in unlimited personal and
client projects under the [TentUI License](/license). The license doesn't
allow redistributing or reselling the component itself.
